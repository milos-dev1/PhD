\noindent\textbf{Novel ECC-Based Security Schemes for E-Banking Systems}\par

\section{1. Overview}\indent This document specifies two original cryptographic schemes proposed in this dissertation: (A) ECC-DTB-AKA --- Device- and Transaction-Bound Authenticated Key Agreement for client-server e-banking communication, and (B) ECC-HISE --- Hierarchical Integrity-preserving Secure Envelope for server-server inter-bank data exchange. Both schemes employ elliptic curve cryptography (ECC) as the foundational primitive, selected for its superior security-per-bit ratio compared to RSA and its widespread adoption in modern financial infrastructure standards including EMV, FIDO2, and TLS 1.3.

\indent The schemes are designed to address distinct vulnerability classes identified in Chapter 1: ECC-DTB-AKA targets authentication and session-key establishment weaknesses in client-server channels (Section 1.2), while ECC-HISE targets data confidentiality, integrity, and auditability weaknesses in server-server settlement and record-transfer channels (Section 1.3).

\section{2. Scheme A: ECC-DTB-AKA}\indent ECC-DTB-AKA establishes a mutually authenticated session key between a banking client C and banking server S, with the session key further bound to a device fingerprint and a transaction nonce to enable per-transaction key derivation without additional round trips.

\subsection{Notation}\indent Let E be an elliptic curve over a finite field with base point G of prime order q. Let H denote SHA-256, and HKDF denote RFC 5869 key derivation. Denote scalar multiplication as [k]G. Entities: Client C with long-term key pair (sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} = [sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}]G); Server S with certificate chain rooted in bank CA; device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\} = H(hardware\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{attributes\}) truncated to 128 bits; ephemeral pairs (ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}) and (ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}) generated per session.

\subsection{Protocol Steps (3-Round Handshake)}\indent Round 1 (Client Init): C generates ephemeral (ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}), samples nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{c\} ← \{0,1\}\textasciicircum{}128, computes t\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} = H(EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} || PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} || device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\} || nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{c\}), and sends MSG1 = (EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\}, nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{c\}, Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}(sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, t\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\})).

\indent Round 2 (Server Response): S verifies Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} and PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} against customer registry. S generates (ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}), samples nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{s\}, computes Z = [ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}]EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\} = [ek\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}]EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, MSK = HKDF(Z || device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\} || nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{c\} || nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{s\} || 'ECC-DTB-AKA-v1', 256), and sends MSG2 = (EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{s\}, Cert\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}(sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, H(EK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\} || nonce\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{s\} || MSK))).

\indent Round 3 (Client Confirm): C verifies Cert\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\} and Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{S\}, recomputes MSK, derives TBK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{txn\} = HKDF(MSK || txn\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{nonce\} || 'TBK', 256) for a specific transaction, and sends MSG3 = (HMAC(TBK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{txn\}, txn\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{nonce\}), Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}(sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{C\}, H(MSK || 'confirm'))).

\indent Upon successful verification, both parties hold MSK (master session key) and can derive transaction-bound keys TBK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{txn\} on demand.

\subsection{Security Properties}\indent Authenticity: Mutual authentication via ECDSA signatures and server certificate validation.

\indent Forward secrecy: Compromise of long-term keys does not reveal past MSK values due to ECDH ephemerals.

\indent Device binding: device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\} included in HKDF salt prevents session transfer to unauthorized devices.

\indent Transaction binding: TBK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{txn\} incorporates txn\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{nonce\}, limiting replay to single transaction context.

\indent Resistance: Replay (nonces + timestamps), MITM (signatures + cert chain), session hijacking (device\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{fp\} binding).

\section{3. Scheme B: ECC-HISE}\indent ECC-HISE provides encrypted, signed, and auditable envelopes for batch financial records transferred between server nodes in an inter-bank or intra-bank settlement network.

\subsection{Key Hierarchy}\indent Level 0 --- Master Key (MK): Stored in HSM at clearing-house authority; MK ∈ Z\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{q\}.

\indent Level 1 --- Domain Keys: DK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{i\} = HKDF([MK]G || domain\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{id\} || 'DK', 256) for each business domain (retail, corporate, settlement).

\indent Level 2 --- Session Keys: SK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{batch\} = HKDF(DK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{i\} || batch\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{id\} || epoch || 'SK', 256) rotated per batch.

\subsection{Envelope Construction}\indent For each record r\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} in batch B = \{r\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{1\}, ..., r\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{n\}\}: (1) Generate ephemeral eph\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}; compute shared Z\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} via ECDH(eph\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}, PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{receiver\}). (2) Derive K\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{enc\} = HKDF(Z\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} || SK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{batch\}, 256); split into K\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{aes\} (128-bit) and K\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{mac\} (128-bit). (3) ciphertext\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} = AES-256-GCM(K\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{aes\}, IV\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}, r\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}). (4) sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} = Ed25519(sk\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{sender\}, H(ciphertext\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} || batch\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{id\} || j)). (5) leaf\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} = H(ciphertext\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} || sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\} || metadata\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}). Merkle root Root = Merkle(leaf\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{1\}, ..., leaf\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{n\}) is included in batch header. Header = (batch\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{id\}, epoch, PK\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{eph\}, Root, Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{header\}, timestamp).

\subsection{Verification and Audit}\indent Receiver verifies Sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{header\}, reconstructs Merkle root from received records, decrypts each record, and validates sig\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{j\}. Any tampering breaks Merkle root or GCM authentication tag. Audit log stores (batch\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{id\}, Root, timestamp) immutably for regulatory compliance.

\section{4. Comparison Baselines}\indent ECC-DTB-AKA will be compared against: TLS 1.3 full handshake + application-layer OTP; standard ECDH without device/transaction binding; OAuth 2.0 PKCE flow.

\indent ECC-HISE will be compared against: TLS record layer only; AES-GCM with static pre-shared keys; standard ECIES without hierarchical keys or Merkle audit chain.

\indent Metrics: authentication latency (ms), ciphertext overhead (bytes), round-trip count, and qualitative attack-resistance matrix.

\section{5. Implementation Mapping}\indent Prototype modules: prototype/ecc\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{dtb\}_aka/ (client, server, protocol), prototype/ecc\textbackslash\{\}textsubscript\{hise\}/ (envelope, merkle, hierarchy), prototype/benchmarks/ (timing and size measurements).

\indent Cryptographic library: Python cryptography (OpenSSL backend), curves: SECP256R1 and Ed25519.
